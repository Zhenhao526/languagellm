# Open-world incumbent grounding study: formal results

This archive crosses an established receiver's initialization with a stable object-surface permutation during open-world cultural transmission. It contains 36 parents, 18 single-new expansion children and 288 transfer runs across nine seeds, two architectures, two surface mappings, two receiver initializations and four transfer supports. All transfer training leaves the double-new meaning `(3,3)` out.

The six replay shards passed with maximum absolute error 0.0. A separate checkpoint audit verified the copied receiver arrays and freshly initialized sender arrays in 144 incumbent-receiver runs. See `combined_audit.json`, `receiver_copy_audit.json` and `validity.md`.

For the receiver-only replacement support, the factorized policy scored −0.250 initially and 1.000 after 3,000 feedback updates under the reverse mapping (9/9 seeds); identity stayed at 1.000. The holistic policy remained at −0.250 in both mappings (0/9). The paired surface-by-initialization interaction at the initial checkpoint was −1.304 (95% paired t interval [−1.663, −0.945]). The supplementary analysis is separate from the original frozen endpoint summary.

The reverse mapping is a tabular label permutation, not a visual model. These results identify a narrow receiver-grounding mechanism and do not establish human-like language generation.
